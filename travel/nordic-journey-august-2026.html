The journey was part of the story

This trip brought together time with family and a route through Nordic cities, mountains and waterways. Between 14 and 25 August 2026, I travelled from London to Denmark, crossed overnight to Norway, continued by rail, boat and bus to Bergen, and then visited Sweden and Finland before returning to London.

I filmed short moments along the way: street scenes through vehicle windows, railway platforms, boats in harbour, water trailing behind a cruise boat, and the view over Bergen from the hillside. Those clips became Window Seat Journeys. This post connects those small views into the wider route.

01 / Denmark · 14–17 August

London to Copenhagen, then time with family

I began with an evening flight from London Heathrow Terminal 5 to Copenhagen on 14 August. The recorded departure was 20:35, with arrival in Copenhagen at 23:20 local time. This first part of the trip was based around staying with relatives in the Dalby/Haslev area rather than checking into a Copenhagen hotel.

Denmark was the starting point for the overland-and-water section of the journey. On 17 August, I continued from Copenhagen to Oslo by overnight ferry. The crossing joined two capital cities while also providing the next night’s accommodation.

Where I stayedWith relatives in the Dalby/Haslev area, followed by a family cabin on the Copenhagen–Oslo overnight ferry. Private home details are omitted.

The overnight crossing

The DFDS crossing in my itinerary departed Copenhagen at 16:30 on 17 August and arrived in Oslo at 10:00 on 18 August. The family cabin meant the night itself formed part of the journey, with arrival in Norway the following morning.

02 / Norway · 18 August

A city day in Oslo

After the ferry arrival, Oslo was my first Norwegian city stop. I stayed at Scandic St. Olavs Plass for the night of 18 August, before the early train departure the next morning.

The Oslo footage records a city seen in motion: tram tracks and overhead wires, cyclists at crossings, modern waterfront buildings, street-side cafés and apartment balconies. The Bjørvika and Opera House area gives some of the clips their distinctive angular architecture. Other street views remain deliberately described at city level where the exact road is not established.

Overnight: 18–19 AugustScandic St. Olavs Plass, Oslo, Norway. This is a record of my accommodation, not a scored hotel review.

03 / Norway · 19 August

Oslo to Bergen, the scenic way

The most detailed transport day of the trip started at Oslo Central Station at 06:25. I followed a self-guided Norway in a Nutshell-style route that linked the Bergen Railway, the Flåm Railway, a fjord cruise, a bus and a final train to Bergen.

Instead of treating Oslo–Bergen as a single transfer, this route turned it into a sequence of places: Myrdal in the mountains, the railway down to Flåm, the waterway to Gudvangen, and the onward connection through Voss.

Myrdal, the Flåm Railway and Kjosfossen

At Myrdal I changed from the Bergen Railway to the Flåm Railway. The waterfall footage includes the viewing stop at Kjosfossen, a landmark on the Myrdal–Flåm route. The platform and walkway are part of the railway experience rather than a street-side city attraction.

Flåm harbour to Gudvangen

Flåm brought a change of pace and transport: railway tracks gave way to a harbour, boats and a mountain backdrop. From there the cruise continued towards Gudvangen. The route passes through Aurlandsfjord and Nærøyfjord; my clips show steep mountain sides, water, boat wake and waterfalls along the journey.

For close-up water and waterfall clips, I use the cruise route as the location rather than claiming a precise point on the fjord. After Gudvangen, the bus to Voss and train to Bergen completed the day.

04 / Norway · 19–20 August

Bergen from the street—and above it

I reached Bergen on the evening of 19 August and stayed at Hotel No13 for one night. The next day combined city travel views with the steep ride on Fløibanen and the panorama from Mount Fløyen.

The red and blue funicular carriages, stepped station platforms and tunnel appear across several Shorts. Looking down the tracks gives a different sense of the city: the harbour and buildings sit below, framed by the green hillside. The lookout footage opens out into the wider Bergen panorama.

Other Bergen clips capture a bakery counter, roadside greenery and city streets through the window. I have kept those descriptions to what the footage shows rather than adding unrecorded restaurant names or hotel experiences.

Overnight: 19–20 AugustHotel No13, Bergen, Norway.

On 20 August I flew from Bergen Flesland to Stockholm Arlanda on Norwegian flight DY842, recorded as departing at 19:35 and arriving at 20:55. The airport clips form the transition between the Norwegian scenery and the Sweden part of the trip.

05 / Sweden · 20–24 August

Stockholm views and a family base in Uppsala

After arriving at Stockholm Arlanda, the Sweden part of the trip was based around a family stay in Uppsala, with time in Stockholm as well. This gave the trip a mix of city sightseeing and time with family, rather than another hotel-to-hotel sequence.

The Stockholm waterfront clip looks across the water towards the skyline. Other Swedish clips show a blue passenger train, quiet street corners, crossings, colourful buildings and balconies. Some of these scenes do not establish whether the camera was in Stockholm or Uppsala, so I do not attach an exact city or street to them.

Sweden base: 20–23 August nightsStaying with family in Uppsala. No hotel review or private address is included.

My next confirmed long-distance connection was the early flight from Stockholm Arlanda to Helsinki on 24 August. The precise local transfers between Uppsala, Stockholm and the airport are not recorded here as completed journeys, so this account does not invent a train line or taxi ride.

06 / Finland · 24–25 August

Helsinki for the final city stop

On 24 August I flew from Stockholm Arlanda to Helsinki Vantaa on Norwegian flight D82603. The recorded departure was 07:35, arriving at 09:30 local time. Helsinki became the final overnight stop before the journey back to London.

The landmark footage includes Helsinki Cathedral at Senate Square and Helsinki Central Railway Station. The cathedral’s columns and the station’s copper-green tower give these clips identifiable locations. The cathedral address is Unioninkatu 29, while the station faces Kaivokatu, with its address at Kaivokatu 1.

There are also railway and underground-platform views from the Helsinki-region leg. The exact station and track section are not established in every clip, so those remain regional travel views rather than precise station guides.

Overnight: 24–25 AugustHelsinki. “Scandic Helsinki Station” is the accommodation name recorded in the trip planning; the final hotel stay needs confirmation, so it is not presented here as a verified hotel review.

The return leg was Helsinki to London Heathrow on 25 August, Finnair flight AY1335, with recorded timings of 14:05–15:10 local time. That closed the loop back to London after twelve calendar days across four Nordic countries.
